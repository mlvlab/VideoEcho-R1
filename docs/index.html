Overview

Abstract

Recent large language models achieve strong performance on complex reasoning tasks, where reinforcement learning with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) has emerged as a leading paradigm for optimizing models on self-generated trajectories. However, the on-policy nature of GRPO bounds the model to the reasoning skills it can already produce, restricting it from learning more advanced capabilities. Prior works inject privileged reasoning traces from a stronger teacher policy to guide training, yet these traces are inherently out of distribution with respect to the student policy. We observe that this mismatch between on-policy and off-policy causes gradient clipping on semantically critical reasoning tokens, ultimately rewarding correct answers while leaving the reasoning that justifies them unlearned. Hence, we propose Echo-GRPO, a framework that lets the model reason in the words it speaks. Rather than imitating low-probability privileged traces from the teacher model, Echo-GRPO rewrites them into the student policy's own idiolect, that is, its own characteristic vocabulary and expression patterns, while preserving their semantics via Dual-Reference Decoding. We instantiate this framework as VideoEcho-R1 for video reasoning distillation, achieving consistent improvements across three multimodal LLM backbones and five benchmarks. Finally, we show that our idiolectal paraphrasing is a plug-in module that consistently improves both RL and supervised fine-tuning frameworks for reasoning distillation, demonstrating that policy-aligned supervision extends beyond GRPO.

Contributions

What’s new

A hidden failure mode

In Mixed-Policy GRPO, trust-region clipping suppresses the gradient on semantically critical tokens of privileged traces: the correct answer is rewarded, but the reasoning that justifies it is never learned.

Echo-GRPO & Dual-Reference Decoding

We paraphrase privileged traces into the student's own idiolect with a product of two references of the initial policy, keeping the teacher's meaning and the student's wording.

VideoEcho-R1 & a plug-in

Consistent gains over GRPO, SFT and Mixed-Policy GRPO on 3 backbones × 5 video benchmarks, and the paraphrasing also improves other RL and SFT distillation frameworks.

Background

Video reasoning distillation with GRPO

GRPO reinforces the model’s own best rollouts, so it is bounded by the reasoning the model can already produce. Mixed-Policy GRPO swaps one rollout for a privileged trace from a stronger teacher. Echo-GRPO paraphrases that trace first.

Motivation

Rewarded for the answer, not the reasoning

GRPO only reinforces reasoning the model can already produce. Mixed-Policy GRPO replaces one rollout with a privileged trace from a stronger teacher (Seed1.5-VL). Here is what that trace looks like inside the update.

Method

Echo-GRPO: reason in the words you speak

Instead of imitating the privileged trace, Echo-GRPO paraphrases it into the student's own idiolect with Dual-Reference Decoding (DRD), then uses the paraphrase in place of the privileged trace in the GRPO group.

\[ \pi_{\text{rewrite}}(y_t\mid y_{<t},v,q,\tilde y, I)=\frac{1}{Z_t}\, \underbrace{\pi_{\theta_0}(y_t\mid y_{<t},v,q,I)}_{\text{distributional reference}}\cdot \underbrace{\operatorname{top\text{-}k}\big(\pi_{\theta_0}(y_t\mid y_{<t},v,q,\tilde y,I)\big)}_{\text{semantic reference}} \]

Semantic reference

The student conditioned on the privileged trace ỹ proposes its top-k next tokens, so the paraphrase stays faithful to the teacher's meaning.

Distributional reference

The student without ỹ scores the full vocabulary, so each token stays likely under the student's own distribution.

Product of experts

A token wins only if both references agree; when they disagree, DRD picks a token preferred by both in 85.6% of cases.

The reasoning is now learned

Grounded reasoning, not imitated format

GT: B. Kidnapper and kidnapped person
Mixed-Policy GRPO → A (neighbor) ✗“…since this is a fictional context, we need to infer based on the provided information. Wait, this is part of a very long series, so the answer is likely based on the specific narrative…”
